# Semantic layer

Built in Week 9 with dbt and MetricFlow. Files: `dbt/models/semantic/` (four views, a time
spine, the generated `_semantic_layer.yml`, the hand-written `_saved_queries.yml`),
`scripts/build_semantic_layer.py`, `tests/test_semantic_layer.py`.

## What a semantic layer is, and why we have one

A semantic layer is **one place where each metric is defined once**: its name, its formula and
what it may be grouped by. Every tool then asks for the metric by name and gets the SQL written
for it.

Without it, "GHG intensity of the Asset-heavy group" would be typed again as a DAX measure in
Power BI, as a formula in Excel, as SQL in Streamlit and as a prompt for the AI analyst - four
chances to get four numbers. With it, all four ask for `ghg_intensity_agg` grouped by
`company_year__sector_group` and get the same number.

How it is put together:

```
 dbt/seeds/kpi_catalogue.csv          one row per metric (label, formula, unit, public or not)
        |  python scripts/build_semantic_layer.py
        v
 dbt/models/semantic/_semantic_layer.yml     semantic models + metrics (generated, do not edit)
        |  dbt parse
        v
 dbt/target/semantic_manifest.json
        |  mf query --metrics ... --group-by ...
        v
 SQL on DuckDB  ->  the number
```

- **The catalogue is the single source.** To add or change a metric, edit
  `kpi_catalogue.csv` and run `.\scripts\dev.ps1 semantic`. A test fails if the YAML or the
  table on this page no longer matches the seed.
- **Semantic models** sit on four thin views of the gold tables: `sem_company_year`
  (`fct_company_year`), `sem_disclosure_quality` (`fct_disclosure_quality`), `sem_restatement`
  (`fct_restatement`) and `sem_company` (`dim_company`). The views add only what MetricFlow
  needs: a key for the company-year and the financial year as a date.
- **Entities** (what the tables are joined on): `company` (the ISIN) and `company_year`.
- **Dimensions** (what a metric can be grouped by) are defined once, on `sem_company_year`,
  and reach every metric through the `company_year` entity:

| Group by | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `company_year__fiscal_year_label` | financial year as text, for example FY2025-26 |
| `company_year__peer_group` | one of the nine peer groups |
| `company_year__sector_group` | Financials, Asset-light or Asset-heavy |
| `company_year__ranking_group` | the group a company is ranked in |
| `company_year__size_band` | Large, Mid or Small |
| `company_year__ownership_type` | Government, Private Indian or MNC subsidiary |
| `company__display_name`, `company__symbol` | one company |
| `metric_time__year` | the time dimension (see below) |

  Extra dimensions for some metrics: `disclosure_quality__dqs_grade`,
  `disclosure_quality__is_provisional`, `disclosure_quality__assurance_level`,
  `disclosure_quality__assurance_scope`; `restatement__metric_id`,
  `restatement__classification`, `restatement__explained_by`.
- **Time.** The time dimension is the **last day of the financial year (31 March)**, by year.
  MetricFlow shows a year as its first day, so `metric_time__year = 2026-01-01` means the
  financial year that ended on 31 March 2026, FY2025-26. For anything a person will read, group
  by `company_year__fiscal_year_label`. A restatement belongs to the year of the value that was
  restated, not to the year of the report that restates it. `time_spine_day` is the daily
  calendar table MetricFlow requires.

## Whole group or typical company: `_agg` and `_median`

There are two honest ways to give one intensity for a group, and they answer different questions.

| | `..._agg` (ratio of sums) | `..._median` (typical company) |
|---|---|---|
| Formula | sum of the numerators / sum of the denominators | middle value of the company intensities |
| Question | "How intensive is the group as a whole?" | "How intensive is a typical company of the group?" |
| Weight | large companies weigh more | every company weighs the same |
| Moves when | one large company changes | most companies change |

**A worked example.** Three companies:

| Company | Emissions (tCO2e) | Turnover (Rs crore) | Intensity |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (a power producer) | 900,000 | 500 | 1,800 |
| B | 2,000 | 1,000 | 2 |
| C | 500 | 500 | 1 |

- Whole group: (900,000 + 2,000 + 500) / (500 + 1,000 + 500) = **451**. The group really emits
  451 tonnes for every Rs crore it earns.
- Typical company: the middle of 1,800, 2 and 1 is **2**. A company picked at random looks like B.
- The simple average of the three intensities is 601. It is neither: it gives the small company C
  the same say as A in a figure that claims to describe the group's emissions. **The semantic
  layer has no average of company intensities, and the generator refuses to create one.**

**The real data, FY2025-26, GHG intensity (tCO2e per Rs crore):**

| Sector group | Companies | Whole group (`ghg_intensity_agg`) | Typical company (`ghg_intensity_median`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asset-heavy | 21 | 248.81 | 11.40 |
| Asset-light | 18 | 3.81 | 4.18 |
| Financials | 12 | 0.95 | 0.79 |

In Asset-heavy the two differ by a factor of 22. Both are right. The group holds a few very
carbon-heavy businesses - a power generator above 1,800 and cement and steel makers from about
475 to about 1,120 tonnes per Rs crore - next to many companies below 20. The few carry most of the
emissions, so they set the whole-group figure; the many set the median. Use `_agg` for "what
does this part of the economy emit per rupee" and `_median` for "what does a normal company of
this group look like". Where the two are close (Asset-light, Financials) the group is even.

Two details that keep `_agg` right:

- numerator and denominator are summed **over the same companies** - those that have both. A
  company with turnover but no emissions figure is left out of both sums, not only of the top;
- percentages are on a 0-100 scale, like every percent column in the warehouse.

## What may be published

Every metric carries `meta.public_safe`, copied from the catalogue
([publication_policy.md](publication_policy.md)). Three rules:

1. **Raw filed totals are never a public metric.** Sums of emissions, energy, water, waste,
   turnover or headcount exist only as the hidden building blocks of a ratio.
2. **A group figure built on filed values is public only for 5 companies or more.** Every
   `_agg` and `_median` metric on the company-year table has `meta.min_companies: 5` and
   `meta.companies_metric`: the metric that counts the companies behind it.
3. **The consumer enforces rule 2.** MetricFlow returns every row. A tool that publishes must
   ask for the companies metric next to the figure and drop the rows where it is below 5.

The saved query `public_rule_min_5_companies` shows it: it returns `ghg_intensity_agg`,
`ghg_intensity_median` and `companies_with_ghg_intensity` by ranking group and year. In three
years Automobiles has 3 or 4 companies; those rows must be dropped before publishing, and a
test (`test_saved_query_shows_the_five_company_rule`) checks exactly that.

```sql
-- what a publishing tool does with the result of the saved query
select *
from saved_query_result
where companies_with_ghg_intensity >= 5
```

Grouping by one company is a local use. A company's own computed intensity is public through
`rpt_public_company_year`; publish it from there, not from the semantic layer.

## How to run it (Windows, PowerShell)

```powershell
.\scripts\dev.ps1 dbt-build      # builds the warehouse, the views and the time spine
.\scripts\dev.ps1 semantic       # regenerates the YAML, runs dbt parse and mf validate-configs

cd dbt
$env:PYTHONUTF8 = '1'            # needed on Windows: mf prints symbols the console cannot encode

mf list metrics
mf list dimensions --metrics dqs_avg

mf query --saved-query ghg_intensity_by_sector_year

mf query --metrics ghg_intensity_agg,ghg_intensity_median,companies_with_ghg_intensity `
         --group-by company_year__sector_group,company_year__fiscal_year_label `
         --order company_year__sector_group

mf query --metrics dqs_median --group-by company_year__ranking_group `
         --where "{{ Dimension('company_year__fiscal_year_label') }} = 'FY2024-25'"

mf query --metrics material_restatement_share --group-by metric_time__year --explain   # shows the SQL
mf query --metrics dqs_avg --group-by company__display_name --csv out.csv              # to a file
```

`mf` reads `dbt/target/semantic_manifest.json`. Run `dbt parse` (or `dev.ps1 semantic`) after
any change to the YAML, otherwise `mf` works from the old definitions or stops with an error.

## How we know the numbers are right

- `mf validate-configs` checks the definitions and runs every semantic model, dimension, entity
  and metric against the warehouse: 0 errors, 0 warnings. It also runs in CI on the synthetic
  data.
- `tests/test_semantic_layer.py` asks MetricFlow for nine metrics by sector group and year (two
  ratios of sums, a median, three Disclosure Quality metrics, two restatement metrics and a
  count) and compares each with the same figure written by hand in SQL on the gold tables: 102
  values, all equal within 1e-9. This needs the real data, so it runs on the laptop, not in CI.
- A dbt test (`assert_semantic_views_match_facts`) checks that the four views have exactly the
  rows of the tables they read.

## Limitations

- **The publication rule is not enforced by the layer.** MetricFlow has no "hide small groups"
  switch; rule 2 is metadata plus a duty of the consuming tool. Until a tool is built, only
  people run queries.
- **`mf` is a local command-line tool.** There is no server: Power BI and Excel cannot call the
  semantic layer live. They will read tables exported from saved queries (Week 12); the
  definitions stay in one place, the numbers are copied.
- **Year granularity only.** The data is annual; quarter or month questions have no answer. The
  year is shown as 1 January of the calendar year in which the financial year ends.
- **Restatement metrics follow the year of the restated value**, so the latest year has none yet.
- **Disclosure Quality metrics must be compared within one financial year**
  ([disclosure_quality_score.md](disclosure_quality_score.md)); an average over several years
  mixes scores built from different parts.
- **Group figures keep the caveats of the company figures**: inferred units in FY2022-23,
  turnover as the only denominator, today's NIFTY 50 applied to all four years.
- **Partial parsing is switched off in dbt** (`dbt_project.yml`). With metrics attached to models,
  dbt 1.12.5 fails some partial re-parses with a false "two metrics with the name ..." error; a
  full parse on every run costs about 20 seconds.
- **Databricks is a stretch goal and was not done.** The layer runs on DuckDB only.
- **Versions**: dbt-core 1.12.5, dbt-duckdb 1.11.0, dbt-metricflow 0.15.0 (MetricFlow 0.213.0),
  with the semantic-layer YAML spec of dbt 1.12 (semantic model under the model, entities and
  dimensions under the columns, simple metrics instead of measures).
